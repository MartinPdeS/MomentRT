Choose a transport model#

MomentRT provides steady-state, monochromatic moment approximations. Select the model from the physical domain and desired result.

Start with your question#

  • What is the fluence around an embedded point source? Use InfiniteP1 for an infinite homogeneous medium. Its Cartesian mesh is an observation window, and the analytical solution is singular at the source. It cannot predict escape through a physical surface.

  • What are the fluence and escaping power in a finite sample? Start with P1 when a P1 approximation is appropriate. Define the sample, coupled diffuse surface source, mesh, and all six face conditions explicitly. Consider SP3 or P3 when their additional angular moments and supported boundary conditions are relevant to the question.

  • How much power reaches a contact aperture? Add a SurfaceDetector to a finite simulation and run a detector convergence study. Detector power uses a Lambertian angular approximation; per-face escaping power is not an aperture measurement. The model does not include external detector optics or specular entry losses.

For any finite solver, refine the mesh and assess source footprint sampling; for detector power, assess aperture sampling and power stability as well. Area agreement and energy balance alone do not establish convergence or model accuracy. Compare the observable against a suitable transport reference for the intended geometry, particularly near a source or in weakly scattering media. See Meshes and local refinement, Finite-aperture surface detectors, and Choosing a mesh for detector collection.

Check the configuration and its assumptions before solving:

simulation = MomentRT.Simulation(...)
report = simulation.validate()
print(report.summary())

The report lists errors that prevent a solve, mesh and footprint cautions, and model_notes containing the configured solver’s assumptions and known limits. Validation does not solve the equation, establish mesh convergence, or determine whether an approximation is accurate for a particular sample.

Key assumptions and limits

Example

InfiniteP1

A homogeneous, unbounded medium with an embedded isotropic point source.

Analytical P1 Green function; no physical boundaries or finite source. Singular at the source; diffusion is least reliable nearby. No FEM solve.

Infinite medium

P1

A finite sample illuminated by diffuse light on a face.

P1 diffusion with continuous fluence, centroid-sampled properties, and explicit six-face conditions. Internal refractive-index jumps are rejected.

Finite sample

SP3

A finite sample when the P1 closure is too restrictive and a higher angular moment model is useful.

Simplified P3; requires phase-function moments, zero-reflectance Robin conditions on nonreflecting faces, and equal internal indices. Unilluminated faces may be reflecting. Two coupled FEM fields; it is not full P3 or the radiative transfer equation.

P1 and SP3

P3

A finite sample when retaining all three-dimensional harmonics through degree three is useful.

Full P3 moment approximation; requires phase-function moments and matched-index zero-reflectance Robin faces. Six coupled FEM fields can cost substantially more than SP3. More angular detail does not guarantee accuracy for a given geometry.

P1, SP3, and P3

P1 diffusion is generally appropriate when multiple scattering supports a diffusive description and the observable is not dominated by the source boundary layer. Higher-order moment models retain more angular information, but remain approximations and have their own boundary and phase-function limits. For optically thin, weakly scattering, strongly absorbing, or very short source-detector configurations, compare against a suitable radiative transfer or Monte Carlo reference rather than assuming a higher order is accurate.

Model notes are available programmatically as report.model_notes. The complete report also identifies unsupported internal refractive-index jumps with region names and resolved indices. Fixing configuration errors does not remove numerical or physical model error.

Independent references#

The planar slab example compares P1 and SP3 finite-element fields with independently calculated one-dimensional analytical solutions. This checks the FEM implementation and spatial discretization for a planar homogeneous case. It does not validate the approximations against the full radiative transfer equation.

The sensor validation benchmark compares measured aperture power from P1, SP3, and P3 against an independent layered-slab photon transport reference across source-detector separations and mesh refinements.

For a broader view of model breakdown in scattering slabs, see Akdemir et al., Breakdown of light transport models in photonic scattering slabs with strong absorption and anisotropy. Its Monte Carlo comparisons show why P1 and P3 applicability depends on optical thickness, absorption, anisotropy, and refractive-index contrast. Those results are not a direct validation of MomentRT’s finite-element implementation.
