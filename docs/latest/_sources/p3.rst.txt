Full three-dimensional P3 transport
====================================

``P3`` approximates the angular radiance using all real spherical harmonics
through degree three. In three dimensions there are 16 coefficients: one of
degree zero, three of degree one, five of degree two, and seven of degree
three. This is distinct from :doc:`sp3`, whose two scalar equations simplify
the multidimensional angular structure. Neither approximation recovers the
full radiative-transfer solution in every configuration.

Angular representation and coefficients
---------------------------------------

For real orthonormal harmonics :math:`Y_{lm}` on the unit sphere,

.. math::

   L(\mathbf r,\mathbf\Omega) \simeq
   \sum_{l=0}^{3}\sum_{m=-l}^{l} c_{lm}(\mathbf r)Y_{lm}(\mathbf\Omega).

MomentRT orders each degree by :math:`m=-l,\ldots,l`; negative orders use
sine and positive orders cosine with the Condon–Shortley convention. The
six coefficients of degrees zero and two form :math:`\mathbf c_e`; the ten
coefficients of degrees one and three form :math:`\mathbf c_o`. Fluence is
:math:`\Phi=\sqrt{4\pi}\,c_{00}`.

For each material's normalized phase-function moments :math:`g_l` and reduced
scattering coefficient :math:`\mu_s'=\mu_s(1-g_1)`, the collision coefficient
for degree :math:`l` is

.. math::

   \sigma_l = \mu_a + \mu_s'\frac{1-g_l}{1-g_1},\qquad l=0,1,2,3.

Thus :math:`\sigma_0=\mu_a`. For Henyey–Greenstein :math:`g_l=g^l`.
Coefficients are evaluated per material at the
source wavelength and sampled at tetrahedron centroids. No assumption that
different materials share an anisotropy is needed. Internal refractive-index
jumps are currently rejected.

Even-parity formulation
-----------------------

Define the angular streaming matrices and diagonal collision matrices by

.. math::

   (A_i)_{oe} &= \int_{4\pi} Y_o\Omega_iY_e\,d\Omega,\\
   \Sigma_e &= \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_0,\sigma_2,\ldots,\sigma_2),
   \qquad
   \Sigma_o = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_3).

With no internal source, the odd moment equation can be eliminated locally:

.. math::

   \mathbf c_o = -\Sigma_o^{-1}\sum_i A_i\partial_i\mathbf c_e.

Substitution into the even equations gives six coupled second-order
equations, with the tensor coupling retained in three dimensions:

.. math::

   -\sum_{i,j}\partial_i\!\left(A_i^T\Sigma_o^{-1}A_j
   \partial_j\mathbf c_e\right)+\Sigma_e\mathbf c_e=0.

Each even moment uses the same continuous linear tetrahedral basis. The ten
odd coefficients are reconstructed at element centers; they can be
discontinuous across element or material boundaries. The reported
``result.flux`` is the first angular moment reconstructed there. The six
nodal even coefficients are available in ``result.moments`` under names
such as ``l0_m0`` and ``l2_m0``. Their values follow the normalized harmonic
convention above; they are not themselves the SP3 composite moments.

Matched-index boundary and illumination
----------------------------------------

For outward normal :math:`\mathbf n`, the current of the even test moments
is :math:`\mathbf q_n=\sum_i n_i A_i^T\mathbf c_o`. Project the prescribed
incoming radiance onto the incoming half sphere using the six weights
:math:`(\mathbf\Omega\cdot\mathbf n)Y_e`. For an isotropic incoming
hemisphere with partial current :math:`I_{\rm in}`, its radiance is
:math:`I_{\rm in}/\pi`. Define

.. math::

   B_{ef} &= 2\int_{\mathbf\Omega\cdot\mathbf n>0}
   (\mathbf\Omega\cdot\mathbf n)Y_eY_f\,d\Omega,\\
   b_e &= \frac1\pi\int_{\mathbf\Omega\cdot\mathbf n>0}
   (\mathbf\Omega\cdot\mathbf n)Y_e\,d\Omega.

Parity of the even and odd harmonics then gives the matched-index Marshak
condition

.. math::

   \mathbf q_n = B\mathbf c_e-2I_{\rm in}\mathbf b.

The weak form adds :math:`B` as a boundary mass matrix and
:math:`2I_{\rm in}\mathbf b` as the source load. Angular matrices are
evaluated using Gauss–Legendre and equally spaced azimuthal quadrature; the
chosen rules integrate the required P3 polynomial products. In planar
geometry the azimuthally symmetric P3 coefficients reduce to the familiar
two composite equations and Marshak coefficients documented in :doc:`sp3`.

All six faces currently require ``RobinBoundary(effective_reflectance=0)``.
Reflecting boundaries and index-mismatched or refracting interfaces are not
implemented for P3 and are rejected explicitly. Surface sources specify
incoming diffuse power already coupled into the sample; collimated fiber
propagation and entry losses are outside this model.

The outgoing face partial current is the reconstructed net normal current
plus the incoming partial current. The existing contact detector integrates
that escaping field over its aperture and applies its **separate Lambertian
angular-acceptance approximation**. It does not yet integrate the reconstructed
P3 radiance over a fiber's acceptance cone, and does not model fiber modes or
Fresnel transmission.

Numerical checks and limits
---------------------------

Independent planar matrix-exponential slab solutions check the P3 reduction,
boundary source, and spatial refinement. A three-dimensional nearly
non-scattering cube is compared with an independent ballistic ray integral;
its nonzero discrepancy is expected because P3 is a truncated angular model.
Tests also exercise axis rotation, energy balance, spectral preparation,
detector quadrature, and unsupported configurations. These cases do not
establish accuracy for skin, all optical properties, or short source–detector
separations. Compare the intended sensor geometry against Monte Carlo or a
converged transport reference and assess spatial mesh convergence separately.

Higher-order angular truncations can produce negative reconstructed fluence
or outgoing irradiance. MomentRT preserves these values and reports them;
it does not silently clip them. P3 has six coupled nodal unknowns rather than
SP3's two and can require substantially more memory and computation. Actual
cost depends on geometry, mesh, and sparse-factorization fill-in.

As one illustrative local measurement, a homogeneous 4 mm cube with a
9 × 9 × 9 mesh, uniform-face illumination, and direct sparse solves gave:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Model
     - Solve time
     - Process peak RSS
   * - P1
     - 0.032 s
     - 161 MiB
   * - SP3
     - 0.039 s
     - 164 MiB
   * - P3
     - 0.662 s
     - 208 MiB

Each model ran in a fresh process; peak RSS includes the interpreter and
dependencies. These are single-run macOS measurements, not performance
guarantees. Larger meshes can change both ratios and factorization memory
substantially.

See :doc:`gallery/transport_models/compare_p3` for a runnable comparison. For
the finite-element P\ :sub:`N` approach in optical tomography, see
`Mohan et al., Journal of Computational Physics 230 (2011), 7364–7383
<https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2011.06.004>`_.
