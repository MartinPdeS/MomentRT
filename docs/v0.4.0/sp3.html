SP3 transport model#

The SP3 solver solves a steady-state simplified third-order moment model on the same finite tetrahedral mesh as Diffusion. Its unknowns are two coupled composite moments. In planar one-dimensional geometry these equations coincide with the corresponding P3 moment equations; in three dimensions SP3 is a simplification of P3, not the full spherical-harmonics system.

Scattering and bulk equations#

The first reduced scattering coefficient alone does not determine the higher angular moments needed by SP3. Each material therefore requires an explicit, dimensionless anisotropy \(g\) in \((-1,1)\). The current solver assumes a Henyey–Greenstein phase function in every region. Different materials can have different values; SpectralMaterial can also interpolate anisotropy across wavelengths. Materials expose the current choice as phase_function="henyey_greenstein". With absorption \(\mu_a\) and reduced scattering \(\mu'_s = \mu_s(1-g)\), define

\[\begin{split}\mu_1 &= \mu_a + \mu'_s,\\ \mu_2 &= \mu_a + \mu'_s(1+g),\\ \mu_3 &= \mu_a + \mu'_s(1+g+g^2),\\ D_1 &= (3\mu_1)^{-1},\qquad D_2 = (7\mu_3)^{-1}.\end{split}\]

The two composite nodal fields \(u\) and \(v\) obey

\[\begin{split}-\nabla\!\cdot(D_1\nabla u) + \mu_a u - \tfrac23\mu_a v &= 0,\\ -\nabla\!\cdot(D_2\nabla v) - \tfrac23\mu_a u + \tfrac19(4\mu_a+5\mu_2)v &= 0.\end{split}\]

Each coefficient is evaluated with the anisotropy of the local material at the source wavelength. The physical fluence and first angular current are

\[\Phi = u - \tfrac23 v,\qquad \mathbf J=-D_1\nabla u.\]

Both composite fields are available as result.moments['composite_1'] and result.moments['composite_2']. result.fluence contains \(\Phi\), and result.flux contains element-centered \(\mathbf J\).

Marshak face conditions#

Let \(j_1=-D_1\mathbf n\cdot\nabla u\) and \(j_2=-D_2\mathbf n\cdot\nabla v\), with \(\mathbf n\) pointing outward. On a nonreflecting, matched-index face, the incoming radiance is assumed constant over the inward hemisphere. Its partial current is \(I_{\mathrm{in}}\). To derive the face conditions, use the local planar angular expansion in \(\mu=\mathbf\Omega\cdot\mathbf n\):

\[L(\mu)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\Phi+3\mu j_1 +\frac53 v P_2(\mu)+7j_2P_3(\mu)\right].\]

The second angular moment is \(v/3\). For a constant prescribed incoming radiance, the two half-range conditions are

\[\begin{split}\int_{\mu<0}(-\mu)L\,d\Omega &= \tfrac14u-\tfrac1{16}v-\tfrac12j_1=I_{\mathrm{in}},\\ \int_{\mu<0}P_3(\mu)L\,d\Omega &= \tfrac1{16}u-\tfrac7{48}v+\tfrac12j_2 =\tfrac14I_{\mathrm{in}}.\end{split}\]

Solving these two equations for the outward currents gives

\[\begin{split}j_1 &= \tfrac12 u-\tfrac18 v-2I_{\mathrm{in}},\\ j_2 &= -\tfrac18 u+\tfrac7{24}v+\tfrac12 I_{\mathrm{in}}.\end{split}\]

The second source term follows from integrating \((5|\mu|^3-3|\mu|)\) over the inward hemisphere. It cannot be inferred from incoming power for an arbitrary angular distribution; the isotropic incoming assumption fixes it. UniformSurfaceSource and SurfaceSource use their usual spatial footprints and coupled optical power, now with this explicit angular assumption. A reflecting face has \(j_1=j_2=0\) and cannot be illuminated.

Only RobinBoundary(effective_reflectance=0) is supported by SP3. A single P1 effective reflectance does not specify the higher angular reflection moments required by an SP3 refractive boundary. Nonzero values are rejected, as are internal refractive-index jumps. No index matching is inferred from the material’s numerical refractive index: the user explicitly chooses the matched-index boundary model.

On a nonreflecting face the outgoing partial current is \(j_1+I_{\mathrm{in}} = u/2-v/8-I_{\mathrm{in}}\). These values are integrated at boundary quadrature points for face powers and detector apertures. Detector angular acceptance continues to use MomentRT’s separate Lambertian escape approximation; SP3 does not provide Fresnel-resolved detector optics. Negative fields are retained and reported rather than clipped.

Finite-element workflow#

Multiplying each bulk equation by a nodal test function and integrating by parts gives two diffusion stiffness matrices, three bulk mass terms, and a coupled boundary mass matrix with coefficients \(1/2\), \(-1/8\), and \(7/24\). The boundary load is \((2I_{\mathrm{in}}, -I_{\mathrm{in}}/2)\). The resulting symmetric sparse system has twice as many nodal unknowns as P1. Material coefficients are sampled at tetrahedron centroids; source and detector footprints use boundary triangle quadrature. Spectral sweeps reuse the geometry and quadrature, then assemble and solve a new coupled operator at each wavelength.

The test suite independently solves homogeneous and layered planar SP3 systems using a matrix exponential and checks mesh refinement, outgoing power, and energy balance. This validates the implementation against that SP3 problem; it does not validate SP3 against radiative transfer or biological measurements. Check mesh convergence, and compare against Monte Carlo or other transport references for the intended sensor distances and tissue properties.

For runnable examples, see Use different anisotropies in a layered sample for material-specific anisotropy and Sweep wavelength-dependent scattering anisotropy for a wavelength sweep.

Reference: H. K. Kim et al., Frequency-domain optical tomographic image reconstruction algorithm with the simplified spherical harmonics (SP3) light propagation model, International Journal of Thermal Sciences 116 (2017), 265–277. Author-hosted paper.
