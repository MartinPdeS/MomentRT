Results and visualization#

Simulation results keep fields, units, and provenance together. Plotting is a view of the computed arrays; it does not modify the numerical result.

Fluence rate#

result.fluence is a ScalarField of nodal fluence rate \(\Phi\), with units of watts per square metre. values is a Pint quantity shaped like its associated Cartesian mesh. Infinite and finite solutions also carry metadata describing the transport model, wavelength, and source configuration.

The default Plotly view uses a volume trace. Its opacity slider changes the volume transparency in the browser; opacity=... chooses the starting value. scale="log" colors points by \(\log_{10}(\Phi)\) while hover labels and stored values remain in physical units. Log rendering rejects nonpositive data instead of silently clipping it.

Other scalar-field views are:

  • view="slices" shows the actual central grid plane along each Cartesian axis. Values are not interpolated onto arbitrary section locations.

  • view="isosurfaces" displays surfaces of constant field value. This can expose spatial structure, though nested surfaces can occlude each other.

  • view="volume" integrates translucent samples through the volume. It gives an overall spatial impression; crowded or dense grids can take longer to redraw as opacity changes. isosurface_count controls the number of iso levels used for this rendering.

In isosurface mode, isosurface_count sets how many surface levels Plotly extracts between the minimum and maximum display values. A larger count shows more levels and can add detail, though nested surfaces may occlude each other and rendering can take longer. If omitted, MomentRT keeps six levels for view="isosurfaces" and eight for view="volume".

For example:

figure = result.fluence.plot(
    view="volume",
    scale="log",
    opacity=0.12,
    show=False,
)

figure.write_html("fluence.html")

For example, request more constant-fluence surfaces:

figure = result.fluence.plot(
    view="isosurfaces",
    scale="log",
    opacity=0.28,
    isosurface_count=12,
    show=False,
)

The volume view is a rendering of sampled nodal values, not a geometric boundary or a claim that every displayed contour is resolved. Mesh refinement and model validation remain numerical tasks.

Source and detector overlays#

Use overlays to place instrument geometry on the same 3D axes as a scalar field. Sources draw their illumination marker, disk, or full-face patch. Contact detectors draw an aperture and an outward collection axis. For example:

figure = result.fluence.plot(
    view="volume",
    scale="log",
    overlays=[source, reflection_detector, transmission_detector],
    show=False,
)

The sequence order determines drawing and legend order. To choose labels, supply a mapping instead:

figure = result.fluence.plot(
    overlays={
        "Illumination": source,
        "Reflection aperture": reflection_detector,
    },
    length_unit="mm",
    show=False,
)

All overlays use the displayed coordinate unit. Each object’s related traces share a legend group: hiding a detector hides both its aperture and collection axis. The volume opacity slider changes only the fluence trace. Overlays do not change the field, detector power, source configuration, or energy accounting.

Full-face illumination needs physical sample bounds. The finite solver records those bounds in the fluence metadata, so its fields can place a UniformSurfaceSource on the correct face. The observation window of an infinite-medium field is not a sample boundary and is not used as one. Explicit point, disk, and aperture coordinates can be drawn without those physical bounds.

simulation.plot_system() uses the same object-owned _add_to_figure(figure=..., context=...) hooks, keeping source and detector styling consistent across views. These hooks are internal; the public entry point for composing field figures is overlays.

Collected-power spectra use wavelength and power axes, so geometric overlays belong on a constituent spatial field: spectrum.results[2].fluence.plot(overlays=[source, detector]). See the spectral detector example for both figures.

Diffusive flux#

For finite FEM simulations, result.flux is an element-centered VectorField with units of \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\):

\[\mathbf J_e=-D_e\nabla\Phi\vert_e.\]

The gradient is constant inside each linear tetrahedron. Values are not averaged across element faces or optical interfaces. result.flux.plot() shows a sampled set of 3D arrows; max_vectors controls how many arrows are drawn. Their lengths are scaled for visibility and should be read as direction and relative magnitude, not a calibrated length scale.

Sample geometry and sections#

sample.plot() displays the rectangular extent and transparent construction surfaces for layers, boxes, and spheres. This is helpful for checking names, placement, and overlap order, but it is not the resolved Boolean material map. A cross-section is sampled at cell centers and resolves the same insertion-order precedence used by material lookup.

Use sample.section(...) for numerical section data or sample.plot_section(...) for an interactive Plotly view. normal selects the plane normal and position is an absolute coordinate with length units. Resolution gives horizontal and vertical cell counts. Thin features may be missed unless the resolution is fine enough.

Energy and solver diagnostics#

For finite samples, result.energy_balance contains incoming_power, absorbed_power, escaping_power, and residual_power as unit-bearing quantities. result.outgoing_power_by_face separates escaping power by sample face. This is total face-integrated power, not a detector collection efficiency.

Configured Finite-aperture surface detectors have a separate output: result.detectors[name] is a DetectorResult, and result.detector_power[name] is its collected power. The result includes sampled and geometric aperture areas and the angular acceptance fraction. These are passive readouts of the escaping field; they are not subtracted again from the energy balance.

result.boundary_fields[face] contains the boundary quadrature positions, area weights, and escaping irradiance used for collection. Its escaping_power integrates the full face. Boundary arrays are snapshots, so later edits to source or material configurations do not change them.

For a wavelength sweep, the returned spectral result keeps one ordinary result per wavelength in results and stacked detector powers in detector_power. The wavelength vector preserves the requested ordering; the sweep does not integrate these independent powers into a broadband signal.

result.diagnostics includes:

  • linear_relative_residual: relative residual of the sparse algebraic system after solving.

  • minimum_fluence: smallest nodal value; nonpositive values are reported, not clipped.

  • tetrahedron_count: number of linear tetrahedral elements.

  • source_quadrature_area: disk area sampled by triangle quadrature, or the entire face for uniform illumination.

  • source_geometric_area: the configured disk or face area.

  • preparation_reused: whether this solve reused the prepared geometry from an earlier wavelength in the same sweep.

These diagnostics help identify algebraic errors and under-resolved discretization. Energy conservation is necessary for a consistent solve, but it does not establish agreement with radiative transfer or an experiment.

The detector convergence study organizes these area diagnostics and collected-power comparisons across explicit mesh levels. It preserves failed resolution checks and returns no recommended mesh when the supplied fine levels do not satisfy the requested criteria.

Export#

ScalarField.export(path="fluence.vti") writes a VTK ImageData file using optional PyVista. The export stores the scalar values, field units, coordinate units, and field metadata. The numerical solve and Plotly figures do not require PyVista.
