Transport theory and approximations¶

MomentRT currently implements steady-state P1 diffusion. It does not solve the full radiative transfer equation (RTE), a higher-order \(P_N\) system, or an \(SP_N\) approximation. Those models have different angular closures and capabilities. The two implemented solvers use the same diffusion physics in different geometries: an analytical Green function for an infinite homogeneous medium and a finite-domain FEM solve.

Radiative transfer and angular moments¶

Let \(L(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\) denote radiance at position \(\mathbf r\) in direction \(\mathbf s\). A steady, monochromatic transport equation with absorption, scattering, and source \(Q\) can be written

\[\mathbf s\cdot\nabla L + (\mu_a+\mu_s)L = \mu_s \int_{4\pi} p(\mathbf s,\mathbf s') L(\mathbf r,\mathbf s')\,d\Omega' + Q(\mathbf r,\mathbf s).\]

Here \(\mu_a\) is the absorption coefficient, \(\mu_s\) the scattering coefficient, and \(p\) a normalized phase function. The angular moments used in diffusion are the fluence rate and vector current:

\[\Phi(\mathbf r)=\int_{4\pi}L(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\,d\Omega, \qquad \mathbf J(\mathbf r)=\int_{4\pi}\mathbf sL(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\,d\Omega.\]

The transport equation couples these moments to higher angular moments. The P1 closure truncates the angular expansion after the first moment. Equivalently, it approximates radiance by

\[L(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\approx \frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\Phi(\mathbf r)+3\,\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf J(\mathbf r)\right].\]

This closure treats the angular distribution as nearly isotropic, with a first-order directional correction. It does not retain a narrow beam or resolve strongly directional propagation.

Diffusion equation¶

For a source whose first angular moment is zero, the first two moment equations under P1 closure are

\[ \begin{align}\begin{aligned}\nabla\cdot\mathbf J + \mu_a\Phi = q_0,\\\frac{1}{3}\nabla\Phi + (\mu_a+\mu_s')\mathbf J = 0,\end{aligned}\end{align} \]

where \(q_0\) is the isotropic source power per volume and \(\mu_s'=\mu_s(1-g)\) is reduced scattering, with \(g\) the mean cosine of the scattering angle. Eliminating \(\mathbf J\) gives Fick’s law and the scalar diffusion equation:

\[\mathbf J=-D\nabla\Phi, \qquad D=\frac{1}{3(\mu_a+\mu_s')},\]
\[-\nabla\cdot(D\nabla\Phi)+\mu_a\Phi=q_0.\]

MomentRT asks for \(\mu_a\) and \(\mu_s'\) directly, both in inverse length units. It does not infer \(\mu_s'\) from \(\mu_s\) and \(g\). In this convention \(D\) has units of length, \(\Phi\) and \(\mathbf J\) have units of \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), and integrated source and power terms have units of watts.

Infinite homogeneous point source¶

For constant optical coefficients in unbounded three-dimensional space, an isotropic point source with power \(P\) gives

\[-D\nabla^2\Phi+\mu_a\Phi=P\,\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_s).\]

The decaying Green function is

\[\Phi(r)=\frac{P}{4\pi D r}\exp(-\kappa r), \qquad \kappa=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_a}{D}}, \qquad r=\lVert\mathbf r-\mathbf r_s\rVert.\]

InfiniteDiffusion evaluates this expression at every node in a CartesianMesh. The mesh is only a window into the infinite solution. There is no finite-sample boundary condition, interface, or external reflectance in this result. A node exactly at \(r=0\) is rejected because the ideal point source is singular; the model does not silently soften or spread the source.

Diffusion is an approximation¶

P1 is most plausible after scattering has made radiance nearly diffuse and when the field varies over distances larger than the transport mean free path

\[\ell_{\mathrm{tr}}=\frac{1}{\mu_a+\mu_s'}.\]

It becomes less reliable near a point source, near an illuminated boundary, in weakly scattering or optically thin regions, around sharp optical transitions, and when source-detector separation is short. The diffusion law replaces angular detail by a local gradient relation; a smooth-looking field does not establish that the physical field is accurate.

MomentRT does not currently provide a finite-aperture detector model, collimated-source propagation, time dependence, spectral integration, chromophore fitting, or inverse estimation. Refractive index is stored with materials, but no automatic Fresnel rule is derived from it. In the finite solver all internal refractive indices must be equal; an external diffuse boundary coefficient must be supplied explicitly.

What the order labels mean¶

\(P_1\) is the first-order spherical-harmonic angular expansion used to derive diffusion. \(P_N\) denotes a general finite spherical-harmonic expansion through order \(N\); it contains multiple coupled angular moments. \(SP_N\) is a different, simplified system derived from selected even-order transport equations, often arranged as coupled diffusion-like equations. Neither label means that a P1 solve secretly contains P2 or P3. MomentRT currently solves the P1 diffusion equation above, not a P3 or SP3 system.
