Finite-sample P1 solver¶

Diffusion solves for steady fluence rate \(\Phi\) in a finite rectangular sample. The geometry is represented by a structured Cartesian mesh split into linear tetrahedra. All six outer faces receive explicit boundary conditions; the solver does not infer a boundary model from a material’s refractive index.

Continuous equation and face conditions¶

Inside a source-free sample the P1 equation is

\[-\nabla\cdot(D\nabla\Phi)+\mu_a\Phi=0, \qquad \mathbf J=-D\nabla\Phi.\]

For a Robin face, the user supplies an effective diffuse reflectance \(R\in[0,1)\). MomentRT defines

\[\beta=\frac{1-R}{2(1+R)}, \qquad c_R=\frac{2}{1+R}, \qquad \mathbf J\cdot\mathbf n=\beta\Phi-c_R I_{\mathrm{in}},\]

where \(\mathbf n\) points outward and \(I_{\mathrm{in}}\) is incoming diffuse partial-current irradiance already coupled into the sample. This effective coefficient is not normal-incidence Fresnel reflectance. The solver does not choose an index-mismatch formula. A reflecting face uses \(\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf n=0\) and cannot be illuminated.

To derive the finite-element equation, multiply the diffusion equation by a test function \(v\), integrate over the sample \(\Omega\), and integrate the diffusion term by parts. Substituting the Robin condition yields

\[\int_\Omega D\nabla v\cdot\nabla\Phi\,dV +\int_\Omega \mu_a v\Phi\,dV +\int_{\partial\Omega_R}\beta v\Phi\,dA =\int_{\partial\Omega_R}c_R v I_{\mathrm{in}}\,dA.\]

Reflecting faces contribute no boundary matrix or load. The illuminated face must be Robin. For a uniform full-face source, \(I_{\mathrm{in}}=P/A_{\mathrm{face}}\). For a disk source it is a top-hat over the sampled disk area, normalized so its integrated power equals the requested source power.

Finite-element assembly¶

CartesianMesh.from_sample(sample=sample, shape=(n_x, n_y, n_z)) places nodes on the sample faces. Each Cartesian cell is split into six tetrahedra that share a consistent body diagonal. Therefore the total element count is

\[N_{\mathrm{tet}}=6(n_x-1)(n_y-1)(n_z-1).\]

The unknowns are nodal fluence values. On each tetrahedron, the basis functions \(N_i\) are linear and their gradients are constant. Absorption and reduced-scattering properties are sampled at each element centroid, giving constant element coefficients

\[D_e=\frac{1}{3(\mu_{a,e}+\mu'_{s,e})}.\]

The element stiffness and absorption matrices are

\[K^{(e)}_{ij}=D_e V_e\,\nabla N_i\cdot\nabla N_j, \qquad M^{(e)}_{ij}=\mu_{a,e}\frac{V_e}{20}(1+\delta_{ij}).\]

On a boundary triangle with area \(A_f\) and constant \(\beta\), the Robin matrix is

\[B^{(f)}_{ij}=\beta\frac{A_f}{12}(1+\delta_{ij}).\]

These local matrices are assembled into a sparse global system

\[\mathbf A\boldsymbol\Phi=\mathbf b.\]

The source load is integrated with a three-point triangle quadrature rule. For a circular spot, quadrature points outside the disk receive zero irradiance. MomentRT reports the total sampled area. Power normalization ensures the requested integrated power, but it cannot recover a poorly resolved spot edge; refine the mesh and compare results to assess that error.

Material map and interface approximation¶

StructuredMedium first assigns the background, then applies structures in insertion order. Later structures replace earlier ones wherever they overlap. This precedence is used when the solver labels element centroids and samples their material. It does not create an interface-conforming mesh. A layer interface aligned to mesh planes is represented cleanly; an unaligned planar interface or curved sphere is represented only through the centroids of nearby elements. Refine the grid and align important planar boundaries where possible.

The formulation uses one continuous fluence field. MomentRT therefore rejects internal refractive-index jumps rather than implying a refracting interface model. The optical-property map may still vary in absorption and reduced scattering between structures, subject to the geometry and mesh limitations above.

Power accounting and validation¶

The solution is differentiated within each tetrahedron to form element centered current

\[\mathbf J_e=-D_e\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Phi_i\nabla N_i.\]

The returned result.flux stores these vectors without smoothing across interfaces. Absorbed power is integrated from the element absorption and mean nodal fluence. Escaping transmitted partial current is computed per face from the Robin solution, with the incoming contribution on the illuminated face removed. It differs from both gross Robin leakage and net outward current. It is an integral across an entire face, not a detector measurement over an aperture.

The reported balance is

\[P_{\mathrm{in}}=P_{\mathrm{abs}}+P_{\mathrm{escape}}+P_{\mathrm{residual}}.\]

Inspect result.diagnostics for the linear relative residual, minimum fluence, tetrahedron count, and sampled source area. Inspect result.energy_balance for unit-bearing powers. A small residual and good power balance show that the discrete linear system and bookkeeping are consistent. They do not show that P1 approximates RTE well, that a source disk is spatially resolved, or that a mesh is converged. For physical confidence, compare independent references and repeat with refined and interface-aligned meshes.

Limitations to keep visible¶

The finite solver is steady-state and monochromatic. It has no internal Fresnel/refraction law, collimated illumination, detector aperture, time response, spectral integration, or inverse model. P1 diffusion is least reliable near the illumination footprint, near boundaries, in low-scattering areas, around unresolved interfaces, and at short source-to- detector distances. These are limitations of the physical approximation and discretization; changing the plot scale cannot remove them.
